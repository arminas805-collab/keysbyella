# Engineer Sends and Watch Time on Reels

For a ~19-follower faceless piano account, the Instagram playbook for late November 2026 is: **optimise average watch time and sends per reach (likes per reach third), open every reel with the song title or chord name as on-screen text plus audible music in frame 0, and run a few templated series rather than one-off posts.** Instagram names watch time, likes and sends as its top three ranking signals, with sends weighted slightly more for non-follower reach, which is almost all of this account's reach ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/)). Posting hour is a weak lever: studies disagree, and Mosseri says there is no direct link between posting activity and reach ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-answers-creator-questions/744813/)). The strongest niche evidence is that falling-note and top-down tutorials of recognisable songs, chord/voicing explainers, and "every note and finger" classical clips are breaking out for accounts with under 1K followers right now, while 3D marble-music is dominated by 500K+ accounts and costs days per video. Christmas is live now. The book principles (Berger, Heath, Kane, Thompson, Godin, Clear) mostly agree with the platform signals, but they come from summaries, not full reads, so treat them as hypotheses that the daily routine below will test. **There is no published benchmark for accounts this small, no study of 2-3 posts per day, and no faceless-niche dataset**; the plan below is a structured experiment, not a proven recipe.

## What Instagram actually ranks, and what it punishes

The official record is thin but clear. Mosseri's January 2025 statement lists **watch time, likes and sends** as the top three signals and tells creators to check average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/)). Instagram counts both percentage watched and raw seconds watched, so long videos are not penalised ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-longer-video-watch-time-versus-completion-rate/740916/)). Each reel is first shown to a small audience, followers or not, and widens only if that audience responds ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)). Since August 2025, Reels Insights include a per-reel Retention chart and a **skip rate** (the share of views that leave in the first 3 seconds) ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/)). Those two measures turn "good hook" from taste into a number.

Several things are not confirmed and should not drive decisions. Saves, replays, comments and "first-hour velocity" have no official ranking status; treat saves as a diagnostic for educational content and the one-hour check as a leading indicator only. The vendor claim that sends count "3-5x more than likes" is uncited ([SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)). Hook rules such as "motion within 500 ms" are likewise vendor lore.

The penalties are well documented and matter for a repertoire-based account. Reels with visible watermarks are ineligible for recommendation, and identical copies of an original are replaced by the original ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality)). In April 2026 these originality rules were extended to photos and carousels, and accounts that mostly repost lose recommendation eligibility; watermark or speed-change edits do not count as material changes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/)). Practically: export clean from your editor, never re-upload TikTok-watermarked files, and make "two versions per song" genuinely different cuts rather than identical files. Hashtags are capped at 5 per caption and Instagram says a few targeted ones beat many generic ones; Mosseri says hashtags are no longer a primary reach lever ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagrams-implementing-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)). Put the plain-language topic ("Carol of the Bells piano tutorial") in the first caption line and on-screen text instead. The new "AI-generated profile" label targets profiles featuring AI-generated people, and AI-assisted editing or graphics need no label ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/)), so a hands-only or software-visual piano account is unaffected.

On length, Buffer's 2.73M-reel analysis found 90-179 second reels reached **+7.4%** versus each account's own baseline, 30-59s +2.7%, under 15s -1.5%, and over 3 minutes **-49.3%**, which Buffer calls "real but modest" ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/instagram-reels-length)). The niche winners are far shorter (11-72 seconds is typical in the data). Choose 20-60 seconds for tutorials and let hook quality dominate; the length effect is smaller than the hook effect, and no length data exists for accounts under 100 followers.

## Posting windows: start from overlap points and test slowly

The timing studies conflict, so the honest summary is "evenings and weekday late-morning/lunch recur, nothing is universal." Buffer (9.6M posts, all formats, local time) finds Thursday 9 am, Wednesday 12 pm and Wednesday 6 pm best, with Wed/Thu/Tue the best days, Fri/Sat weakest, and 6-11 pm strong most weekdays ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/best-time-to-post-on-instagram)). HubSpot's marketer survey puts Reels best at weekdays 9 am-12 pm with a 6-9 pm secondary window, and has no continental European data ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/instagram-best-time-post)). Metricool's 24.4M-post study peaks at 8-9 pm, but its Reels-specific table is from 2024 and contradicts its own text for some days ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/best-time-to-post-instagram/)). A study of top creators found Tuesday noon-2 pm best for views, with the authors stating no single time fits every category ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/06/20/these-are-the-best-times-and-days-to-post-reels-on-instagram)). None isolates tiny, faceless or piano accounts.

The working schedule below is my inference from the overlap of those sources, designed so each slot is a distinct test. Because ET noon equals 6 pm in Central Europe, one post serves US lunch and European evening at once.

| Slot | US Eastern | US Pacific | Central Europe | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 12:00 | 09:00 | 18:00 | US weekday late-morning/lunch, Europe early evening |
| B | 15:00 | 12:00 | 21:00 | Europe prime evening, US Pacific lunch |
| C | 18:30 | 15:30 | 00:30 | US evening; Europe is asleep, so test it last |

Frequency evidence is equally limited. Buffer found per-post reach rose about 12% at 3-5 posts a week, 18% at 6-9 and 24% at 10+, with diminishing returns, but it pooled formats and never analysed several posts per day ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). Nothing shows that 2-3 reels per day is penalised, and nothing shows it helps beyond that curve. Successful niche accounts do post at volume: pianotype.app published 12 reels in about three days, often two to three variants per song, and results within one account ranged from 131 plays to 127.6K ([Instagram via vidIQ](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DgCqlS4D-yc/)). The sensible reading is that volume buys more lottery tickets, provided each reel is a distinct idea and quality does not collapse. Space posts at least three hours apart and never post two reels of the same song within a day.

## Formats that win now, and which to repeat as multiples

The outlier data (posts beating their creator's own median, late October to late November 2026) points to five formats. Treat the multiples as inflated by tiny medians and biased toward winners, since no failure data was collected.

**Falling-note tutorials of famous songs** are the best fit for a faceless account. @pianofx.app, with 864 followers, got 434.6K views (218x its median) on a "Drowning love" tutorial ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfufMtkyQpg)); @midiforgeapp, with 381 followers, got 65.8K on "Für Elise (easy)" ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfcdeYJjiwf)); @fallingnoteapp's Christmas reel hit 1.3M ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfdkgBWh7Dw)). **Top-down labelled or numbered tutorials** also work: @gnpiano's Einaudi "Experience" reached 2.2M views at 18 seconds ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@gnpiano/video/7707993660532493569)). **Chord and voicing explainers** show the strongest small-account lift: @phrasepiano (270 followers) got 148.7K views, 109.6x median, on "Every major chord on the piano. ONE RULE." ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df95GoXsFet)), and @henning.dampel (1.4K) got 260K on jazz minor-11 voicings ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfsOdH2s9-C)). **Sheet-music plus finger split-screens** of classical hits do well too: @pianotype.app's Moonlight Sonata "every note and its finger" reached 212K ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df0KOfumRNk)). **Satisfying 3D marble music** is real but top-heavy: @satisfy3dverse (596K followers) hit 8.8M with a "Guess all songs" hook ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfcE-UXB6VV)), and its tooling and production time are out of proportion for a one-person account.

My recommendation for repetition is to repeat three formats as multiple similar videos and treat two as occasional experiments. Repeat: (1) the falling-note tutorial of a recognisable song, with one fixed template and new song each time; (2) the "one rule" chord/voicing explainer, numbered; (3) the Christmas falling-note or "every note and finger" clip, as a seasonal block through mid-December. Run jazz (Take Five hit 804.7K on TikTok at 23 seconds ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@keyflowmusic/video/7700234021988535584))) as 2-3 voicing reels a week, and 3D/satisfying as one or two low-cost loop or visualiser pieces a week rather than a production-heavy marble series. Song choice matters more than format: the same account's Carol of the Bells drew 1.9K plays while Canon in D drew 127.6K ([Instagram via vidIQ](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DgCqlS4D-yc/)), so lead with the most familiar titles.

Trending songs and audio are the weakest-evidence area. Buffer's November 2026 Instagram audio list includes Mariah Carey "All I Want For Christmas Is You" and Kelly Clarkson "Underneath the Tree" but no piano-specific tracks ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/trending-audio-instagram/)); no trending licensed piano audio was identified on either platform. Copyright adds a constraint: composition and recording rights are separate layers, Business accounts are restricted to Meta's cleared library or independently licensed music, and escalation runs from muted audio to takedown and strikes ([Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/), a vendor with a commercial interest in stressing risk). No source addresses piano covers and tutorials of pop songs specifically. The lower-risk repertoire is public-domain pieces you perform yourself (Pachelbel, Beethoven, Chopin, Carol of the Bells, First Noel), which is the pianotype approach; for pop, your own recording avoids label recording rights but not publisher rights. That is an inference, and a check with a publisher or your local collecting society is warranted before monetising.

## Book principles that survive contact with the algorithm

The books were studied through summaries, previews and author pages, not read in full, and several key chapters were never retrieved (for example Made to Stick's per-trait detail, This Is Marketing's tension and status ideas, Atomic Habits' habit stacking). Trust Me, I'm Lying and Obviously Awesome were not covered. What follows are applications I derived, not claims the books make.

Seven ideas recur across independent books: familiarity plus surprise (Thompson's "Most Advanced Yet Acceptable" ([Big Think](https://bigthink.com/plus/will-your-product-be-a-hit-or-a-flop-this-simple-rule-can-help-you-decide/)), Heath's Unexpected, Godin's remarkability), emotion, social currency, repeated cues, value before asks, consistency, and concrete simplicity. They map onto the ranking signals. Berger's Social Currency and Practical Value ([Samuel Thomas Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)) are a recipe for sends, so ask of every reel who would send it and what it says about the sender ("send this to the friend who still plays it wrong"). Kane's hook-then-deliver rule ([Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)) and the Heaths' Concrete trait ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)) govern watch time: show the keys or state the specific promise before any intro, and deliver it. Hit Makers' MAYA suggests pairing a very familiar song with a surprising teaching angle. Godin's minimum viable audience ([Seth's Blog](https://seths.blog/2017/07/in-search-of-the-minimum-viable-audience/)) argues for naming one learner type, for instance adults who want to play pop by ear, in the bio and first caption line. Clear's cue-and-reward loop and Kleon's "share something small every day" ([Kleon](https://austinkleon.com/2014/02/19/10-ways-to-share-your-creativity/)) support fixed, numbered series ("Chord Fix #14") with a satisfying finished sound at the end.

Several book tactics do not transfer. Vaynerchuk's "right hook" assumes links in posts and a 2013-era Instagram ([IMPACT](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)); on Reels the ask is "follow for part 2" or a profile link, used rarely. Personal-brand advice (Kleon, Crushing It) leans on a visible person, so a faceless account must substitute a visual and sound signature, consistent caption voice and process footage of hands and score. Cialdini-style scarcity is a trust risk if fake. Hooked's loop depends on notifications and stored data the creator does not control, and the framework has no peer-reviewed support ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)). The comment-keyword CTA ("Comment MOON for the free sheet PDF") ran on every pianotype reel, but comments ran only about 0.1% of plays, so it is lead capture more than algorithm lift.

## Daily routine and a 7-day plan with 2-3 reels per day

### The daily optimisation routine

Run the same loop daily; the thresholds are my working heuristics and no published benchmark supports them. Log for every reel: format tag, hook text, slot, views at 1 hour, 24 hours and 72 hours, reach, skip rate, average watch time and percent watched, sends per reach, saves per reach, likes per reach and follows gained. Rank reels by sends per reach and average watch time first. Compare only against your own baseline, as Buffer does. Scale a format when it sits in your top quartile on sends per reach and watch time after at least three posts with skip rate below your median; rework it when two consecutive posts land in your bottom quartile. Change one variable at a time (hook text, slot or song) and repeat each slot three to four times before concluding, because single-reel variance will exceed any timing effect. Check the Retention chart for where viewers drop and fix that second first. Check whether Trial Reels appears in your app: they show a reel to non-followers first and release insights about 24 hours later ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)), but the follower minimum was not verified from an official source and at 19 followers every reel already reaches mostly non-followers. Spend 15 minutes after each post replying to comments and sending the reel to one relevant community, which follows Instagram's own "send it" logic but is untested here.

Captions follow the same pattern: first line states the topic in plain words, one line names the learner ("for adults who want to play by ear"), one low-friction CTA (save or send to a practice buddy), and at most five targeted hashtags. Alternate CTAs between "send to a friend" and "save to practice" across reels so the metrics reveal which one moves sends per reach. At the end of day 7, apply the decision rules: tag each format by its sends per reach and watch-time quartile, double the winning series, retire the bottom one, and test a new slot.

Two caveats on the plan. First, 18 reels in a week is a volume bet drawn from successful niche accounts, not an evidenced optimum; if quality or the originality of each reel drops, post 10-12 and keep them distinct. Second, the satisfying/3D slots are deliberately cheap visualiser loops, because real 3D marble production is a poor return for one person at this stage.

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is a mismatch between folklore and evidence: hashtags, posting hour and reel length each have weak or conflicting support, while the signals Instagram itself names (watch time, sends, likes) and the measurable skip rate and Retention chart give you something specific to optimise. Small faceless accounts are breaking out right now by applying one clear template to a song people already search for, with the title on screen and music in frame 0, so the bottleneck is repertoire choice and hook testing, not follower count.

What remains unknown is substantial: no signal weights, no data on accounts under 100 followers, no test of multiple daily posts, no verified trending piano audio, no clear copyright treatment of pop tutorials, and book principles known only through summaries. The right posture is to treat the first two weeks as a measured experiment, trust your own quartiles over anyone's benchmarks, and revise the plan on the data you log.
