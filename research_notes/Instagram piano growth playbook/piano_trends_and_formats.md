# Piano, music-education and satisfying-music content trends on Instagram Reels and TikTok (Nov 2026), for a faceless piano account

Method note: most numbers below come from vidIQ's outlier search (posts that beat the creator's own median, window roughly 2026-10-24 to 2026-11-22) and vidIQ's per-account reel pulls. Views = platform plays at time of pull. "xN median" = vidIQ's outlier multiple vs that account's median. Several web searches (fluxnote, Hailun, Buffer-style roundups) returned thin or non-piano content; those were not used except where noted.

Falling-note / visual tutorials of recognisable songs, top-down "chord/voicing" clips with on-screen labels, and "every note and finger" sheet-music split-screens are the clearest winners in Nov 2026, including from accounts under 12K followers. Satisfying 3D marble-music is huge but at the top end (500K+ accounts) and costs days to weeks per video.

## 5. Series formats and posting frequency

### Takeaway

- Variance within one account is large, so posting several per week gives more lottery tickets; no source gives a verified "optimal" frequency.

### Gaps

## 6. Copyright risk for piano covers on Instagram

### Takeaway
Own performances of copyrighted songs still touch the composition (publisher) rights, and platform licences are mainly for private/non-commercial use; enforcement is typically audio muting or takedown. I found no authoritative source specific to piano cover/tutorial risk, so this section is partly a gap.

### Cited Findings
- Two copyright layers: composition (songwriter/publisher) and sound recording (artist/label); a new recording of a public-domain work gets its own recording copyright — [Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/)
- Meta's detection system is Rights Manager; consequences escalate from muted audio (can be retroactive), to removal, DMCA takedown, strikes, restrictions and termination — [Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/)
- Personal accounts: full library for non-commercial; Creator: broad access but sponsored/branded content needs separate license; Business: restricted to Meta Sound Collection (about 14,000 tracks, cleared for FB/IG) or independently licensed music; boosted/paid content needs independently licensed music — [Foxi Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/)
- Osborne Clarke (analysis based on German law, not UK/EU generally): platform libraries are for private users; a personal account may count as commercial if it uses affiliate links or partnerships; licence must cover both composition and recording rights — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/use-music-social-media-platforms-what-businesses-and-creators-need-know-about-usage)
- Foxi Music is a royalty-free music vendor and so has a commercial interest in stressing risk.

### Inferences
- Lower-risk repertoire for the account: public-domain compositions (Pachelbel, Beethoven, Mozart, Chopin, Carol of the Bells, First Noel) performed or synthesised by the user; this is exactly what pianotype is doing. Recording of public-domain pieces made by the user is the user's own, so no third-party recording rights.
- Modern pop songs require care: own recording avoids the label's sound-recording rights but not publisher rights; many tutorials do exist on-platform, so the practical risk is muting/claims rather than lawsuits, but this is an inference, not sourced.
- Do not use the original recording as the audio under a falling-note tutorial of a copyrighted song if monetising or running a business account.
- Several outliers above use the original song or a cover of modern hits at 1M+ views; their survival suggests muted audio is the realistic downside, but I have no evidence on how many were muted.

### Gaps
- No source on how Meta treats piano covers/tutorials of copyrighted songs specifically, or on Meta's agreements with collecting societies (e.g., in the EU) for user-generated covers. Recommend a legal check (music publisher / local collecting society) before monetising.
