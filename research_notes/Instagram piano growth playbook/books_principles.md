# Marketing and Social Media Books: Principles Applied to Faceless Piano-Education Reels

Method note: these notes are drawn from publicly available summaries, author pages and reviews (Shortform previews, Farnam Street, Big Think, Blinkist, personal-site summaries, Wikipedia), NOT from reading the full books. Several summary pages were partial previews, and each gap is flagged. Findings are paraphrased. Items under "Inferences" are my own applications to piano reels, not claims made by the books. Coverage is uneven: the search budget was used on the core books; Trust Me I'm Lying and Obviously Awesome were not read in any depth (see Gaps).

## 1. Per book: 3-5 core principles and the single most useful tactic for a faceless piano reel account

### Takeaway
Across the books, the durable ideas are: (a) make the content worth passing on (Berger, Godin, Thompson); (b) make it instantly clear and concrete (Heath, Miller, Kane); (c) build repeat return through cues and small rewards (Eyal, Clear); (d) give before asking and show process (Vaynerchuk, Kleon); (e) serve a specific small audience (Godin). Each book yields one tactic that fits a faceless, piano-education account (inferences below).

### Cited Findings

**Contagious (Jonah Berger) - STEPPS**
- Social Currency: people share things that make them look good or like insiders; remarkability, game-like comparison mechanics and exclusivity are the levers — [Samuel Thomas Davies summary](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Triggers: environmental cues keep an idea top of mind and lead to action; examples are voting at a school boosting school-funding support and Kit Kat tied to coffee lifting sales by 8 percent — [Samuel Thomas Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Emotion: when people care, they share; high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) drive sharing more than low-arousal ones such as sadness — [Samuel Thomas Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Public: observable things spread; "built to show, built to grow" (Hotmail signature link, Lululemon bags as behavioral residue) — [Samuel Thomas Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Practical Value: people share useful information, which also strengthens social bonds — [Samuel Thomas Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Stories: information travels as casual conversation; the product or point should be so woven into the story that it cannot be retold without it — [Samuel Thomas Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)

**Hooked (Nir Eyal) - Hook Model**
- Four steps: trigger, action, variable reward, investment; one loop is one session — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)
- External triggers (notifications, ads) come first; over time behavior links to internal triggers such as boredom or loneliness — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)
- Action needs enough motivation and ability plus a trigger, and is likelier the easier it is — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)
- Predictable rewards do not create cravings; variable rewards drive the urge to come back — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)
- Investment (inviting friends, storing data, building reputation, learning features) increases value and return visits — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)
- Caveats: the source is a promotional excerpt, the brain-imaging claim is uncited, and the model comes from a business book, not peer-reviewed research — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)

**Made to Stick (Chip and Dan Heath) - SUCCESs**
- Six traits: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)
- Sticky ideas are understandable, memorable and have lasting impact — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)
- Curse of knowledge: experts assume listeners share their knowledge and drift into abstraction; SUCCESs is the counter — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)
- Concreteness example: a movie popcorn's saturated fat was compared to familiar meals so the number meant something — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)
- Unexpectedness: the authors found most award-winning ads fit six templates, mostly built on surprise — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)
- Detail beyond the names of the six traits was not available in the source (see Gaps).

**Influence (Robert Cialdini)**
- Six principles: reciprocation, commitment/consistency, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Reciprocation: going first with a favor prompts a return — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Commitment/consistency: people act in line with stated positions; effort invested raises the value of a thing (IKEA effect) — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Social proof: when unsure, people copy others — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Liking: people are persuaded by those who are similar to them or who like them — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Authority: titles and displays of knowledge trigger deference — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Scarcity: rarity or possible loss raises desire — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Farnam Street page does not mention the later seventh principle, unity — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)

**This Is Marketing (Seth Godin)**
- Do not chase everyone; serve the smallest viable market, defined by shared worldview and wants rather than demographics — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/smallest-viable-market/)
- Minimum viable audience: the smallest market that can sustain you and that you can serve well; focusing improves quality, story and impact, and word of mouth follows — [Seth's Blog](https://seths.blog/2017/07/in-search-of-the-minimum-viable-audience/)
- Successful brands serve a few people deeply and ignore the uninvolved and average — [Seth's Blog](https://seths.blog/2017/07/in-search-of-the-minimum-viable-audience/)
- Other themes of the book (tension, "people like us do things like this", trust through consistency) were not in the retrievable text (see Gaps).

**Purple Cow (Seth Godin)**
- Advertising is less effective amid clutter; both product and marketing must be remarkable ("your one chance is to be remarkable") — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Cow:_Transform_Your_Business_by_Being_Remarkable)
- Remarkability must be built into the product, not bolted on by stunts or ads — [Brikman review](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/02/10/purple-cow/)
- "Safe is risky": merely good gets overlooked; aim to be the most of something — [Brikman review](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/02/10/purple-cow/)
- Seek "otaku" markets (enthusiasts whose interest goes beyond hobby), because standing out there gets talked about — [Brikman review](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/02/10/purple-cow/)
- Criticisms: reviewer found it light on how-to and TV-ad focused; one claim (that advertising is broken) has been challenged — [Brikman review](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/02/10/purple-cow/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Cow:_Transform_Your_Business_by_Being_Remarkable)

**Hit Makers (Derek Thompson)**
- MAYA (Most Advanced Yet Acceptable): hits combine familiarity and surprise; make the familiar feel surprising or the surprising feel familiar — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/plus/will-your-product-be-a-hit-or-a-flop-this-simple-rule-can-help-you-decide/)
- Too far either way flops: Segway and Google Glass were too novel, Zune too similar to the iPod — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/plus/will-your-product-be-a-hit-or-a-flop-this-simple-rule-can-help-you-decide/)
- Star Wars: novel world on a familiar story structure — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/plus/will-your-product-be-a-hit-or-a-flop-this-simple-rule-can-help-you-decide/)
- The book's exposure/distribution arguments were not in the retrievable text (see Gaps).

**Show Your Work! (Austin Kleon)** — ten points — [Kleon's site](https://austinkleon.com/2014/02/19/10-ways-to-share-your-creativity/)
- You do not need to be a genius; voice develops through sharing
- Process over product: people want to see how work is made
- Share something small every day
- Open your "cabinet of curiosities" (influences and tastes)
- Tell good stories about the work
- Teach what you know (tutorials, step-by-step guides)
- Do not become human spam: listen, be a fan of others, avoid constant asks
- Learn to take a punch (criticism grows with exposure)
- "Sell out" (ambition is fine, take opportunities that let you do more of your work)
- Stick around (persist)

**Building a StoryBrand (Donald Miller) - SB7**
- Seven parts: hero's desire, problem, guide, plan, call to action, stakes of inaction, success — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=27392)
- The customer is the hero; the brand is the guide — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=27392)
- Clarity first: cut anything that does not advance the story; people should grasp what it is, why it matters and how to get it within about five seconds — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=27392)
- Show stakes (risk of inaction and benefit of acting) and give one clear call to action — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=27392)

**Hook Point (Brendan Kane)**
- A hook point is a device that grabs attention near-instantly (phrase, visual, personality, distinctive selling point); the premise is a "3-second world" — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- Distinction from clickbait: a hook grabs attention, then delivers real substance — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- Hooks must be refreshed as they lose edge (Nike example) — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- A hook often pairs a relatable truth with an unexpected angle (copywriting example) — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- The Blinkist page did not lay out a formal multi-part hook framework (see Gaps).

**Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook (Gary Vaynerchuk)**
- Jabs are free, useful content that builds trust; the right hook is the ask, kept simple and platform-suited — [IMPACT summary](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)
- Five traits of good social content: native, does not interrupt, does not demand often, micro, consistent and self-aware — [IMPACT summary](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)
- Context matters as much as content; tailoring to each platform beats cross-posting — [IMPACT summary](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)
- Stories are the main connection tool; aim for likeable, shareable content that earns comments — [IMPACT summary](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)
- Instagram is described as visual-first, with long-term payoff and no links in posts, so more jabs are needed (2013-era platform description) — [IMPACT summary](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)

**Atomic Habits (James Clear)**
- Four laws for building a habit: make the cue obvious, the craving attractive, the response easy, the reward satisfying (reverse them to break one) — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary)
- Habit loop: cue, craving, response, reward — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary)
- Identity: each action is a vote for the type of person you want to be; build evidence through small wins — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary)
- 1% gains compound; systems matter more than goals — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary)
- Habit stacking, two-minute rule and environment design were not in the retrievable page (see Gaps).

**Crushing It (Gary Vaynerchuk)**
- Personal brand as a path to opportunity; document your learning rather than posing as an expert — [Life Club summary](https://lifeclub.org/books/crushing-it-gary-vaynerchuk-review-summary)
- Authenticity ("people can smell a fake") and patience; results often arrive slowly — [Life Club summary](https://lifeclub.org/books/crushing-it-gary-vaynerchuk-review-summary)
- Choose platforms deliberately and post often; monetization comes via sponsors, partnerships, products and similar — [Life Club summary](https://lifeclub.org/books/crushing-it-gary-vaynerchuk-review-summary)

### Inferences
Single most useful tactic per book for a faceless piano-education reel account (my application, not a book claim):
- **Contagious:** make every reel pass the "would a pianist or learner send this to a friend?" test. Design for Social Currency plus Practical Value: "send this to the friend who still plays it wrong" or "the 3-chord trick to sound like a pro". A faceless account is built for sends because the content, not a personality, is the object being shared.
- **Hooked:** do not try to build an app-style loop; use the trigger idea. Tie the series to an internal trigger the learner already has ("sitting down to practice and not knowing what to play", "bored after 10 minutes of scales") and a recurring external cue (same weekday, same title format).
- **Made to Stick:** fight the curse of knowledge: replace theory words with a concrete, visible/audible demonstration in the first seconds (a before/after of the same bar, on-screen keys lit up, a named comparison to a famous song).
- **Influence:** use consistency and social proof honestly: pin and reply to learner comments, show "viewers learned this" results (with permission), and invite a small commitment ("try it, then comment your tempo"). Avoid fake scarcity.
- **This Is Marketing:** pick one smallest viable audience (e.g. adult returning pianists who want to play pop by ear) and name them in captions and the bio, rather than "all piano lovers".
- **Purple Cow:** be the most something in the niche (fastest 30-second fix, most visual keyboard overlay, one distinctive recurring sound or visual signature). Faceless accounts need a visual/format signature to be recognisable.
- **Hit Makers:** pair a very familiar song (familiar) with a surprising teaching angle or arrangement (surprise); or a novel technique on a known piece. Do not stray into only-new or only-familiar.
- **Show Your Work!:** share process: practice progress clips, "learning this piece in 7 days", arrangement drafts. Works faceless because hands/keys are the process.
- **StoryBrand:** make the learner the hero: the frame is "you want to play X, the obstacle is Y, here is the plan" and the account is the guide. One clear CTA per reel (save, send, follow for part 2).
- **Hook Point:** write the first-second line and the first visual as one unit, and refresh it when a hook format stops working; the payoff must actually deliver (no clickbait).
- **Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook:** keep roughly a heavy ratio of free tutorials to asks; make the right hook (sheet music, lessons, email list) simple and rare. Make every reel native to Reels (vertical, captions, trending-appropriate audio rules), not repurposed landscape YouTube.
- **Atomic Habits:** apply twice: (1) the creator's own cadence (small, easy, repeatable batch workflow, identity "I am someone who publishes"), and (2) the viewer's: make coming back easy via a fixed series format, and a satisfying micro-win in each reel.
- **Crushing It:** document, do not perform expertise; be patient with slow early results; stay authentic (no fake persona).

### Gaps
- All notes are from summaries; the books themselves were not read. Several pages were partial previews.
- Hooked: Fogg's behavior model and the Hook Model's tribe/hunt/self reward types were not confirmed in retrieved pages.
- Made to Stick: per-trait explanations (Simple/"core", Credible, Emotional) were not in the retrieved text.
- This Is Marketing: tension, status/affiliation ("people like us do things like this"), trust, and change-maker ideas not confirmed in retrieved text.
- Purple Cow: sneezers, Ps of marketing and the ten-point checklist were not confirmed.
- Hit Makers: exposure effect/distribution ideas not confirmed.
- Hook Point: no formal multi-part framework retrieved.
- Atomic Habits: habit stacking, two-minute rule, environment design not on the retrieved page.
- Trust Me I'm Lying and Obviously Awesome: searched, but no page was fetched; no cited findings and therefore not included above. Both are of low relevance (media manipulation; B2B positioning) and can be omitted.
- Show Your Work!, Crushing It and others: only secondary summaries (Kleon's own site for the ten headings; Life Club for Crushing It, whose 2017-era platform advice is dated).

## 2. Which principles agree across books

### Takeaway
Seven themes recur with strong support: familiarity plus surprise, emotion, social currency / identity signalling, repeated cues (triggers), value before asking, consistency/persistence, and concrete simplicity. They map onto the signals Instagram says it ranks on (sends, watch time, likes).

### Cited Findings
- Familiarity + novelty: Thompson's MAYA (familiar with surprise) — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/plus/will-your-product-be-a-hit-or-a-flop-this-simple-rule-can-help-you-decide/); Heath's "Unexpected" — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723); Godin's remarkable/purple cow — [Brikman](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/02/10/purple-cow/); Kane's relatable truth + unexpected angle — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- Emotion: Berger's Emotion (high-arousal) — [Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious); Heath's Emotional — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723)
- Social currency / identity / status: Berger's Social Currency — [Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious); Cialdini's liking and social proof — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/); Clear's identity-based habits — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary); Godin's worldview-defined market — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/smallest-viable-market/)
- Triggers/cues: Berger's Triggers — [Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious); Eyal's triggers — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/); Clear's obvious cue — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary)
- Give value first: Vaynerchuk's jabs — [IMPACT](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk); Cialdini's reciprocation — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/); Kleon's "teach what you know" and "don't be human spam" — [Kleon](https://austinkleon.com/2014/02/19/10-ways-to-share-your-creativity/); Berger's Practical Value — [Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Consistency/persistence: Clear's compounding small gains — [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits-summary); Kleon's share daily and stick around — [Kleon](https://austinkleon.com/2014/02/19/10-ways-to-share-your-creativity/); Vaynerchuk's patience — [Life Club](https://lifeclub.org/books/crushing-it-gary-vaynerchuk-review-summary); Cialdini's consistency — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/influence/)
- Concrete, clear, story-led: Heath's Concrete and Stories — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=45723); Miller's clarity and story arc — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=27392); Berger's Stories — [Davies](https://www.samuelthomasdavies.com/book-summary/business/contagious)
- Narrow audience beats broad: Godin's minimum viable audience — [Seth's Blog](https://seths.blog/2017/07/in-search-of-the-minimum-viable-audience/)
- Process transparency: Kleon (process over product) — [Kleon](https://austinkleon.com/2014/02/19/10-ways-to-share-your-creativity/); Vaynerchuk (document, don't create) — [Life Club](https://lifeclub.org/books/crushing-it-gary-vaynerchuk-review-summary)

### Inferences
- Strongest agreement (3+ books, independent): familiarity+surprise, emotion, give-before-ask, small repeated consistency, concreteness.
- Moderate: social currency/identity, triggers, narrow audience.
- Reasonable mapping to Instagram's own ranking signals (sends, watch time, likes; see section 4): Social Currency and Practical Value feed sends; Hook Point, Heath's Unexpected/Concrete and Miller's clarity feed early watch time; Emotion feeds likes/sends.

### Gaps
- Agreement is based on summaries; nuance (e.g. Berger's emotion findings; Hit Makers' distribution arguments) may be lost.

## 3. Ranked list of the 10 most actionable rules for hooks, captions, series design and posting cadence

### Takeaway
The ranking below is my synthesis (an inference) from the cited principles in sections 1-2 and the platform signals in section 4. Ordered by how directly the rule moves watch time and sends for a faceless piano account.

### Cited Findings
Underlying support for each rule is cited in sections 1, 2 and 4; the combined ranking itself is inference.

### Inferences
1. **Front-load a concrete, surprising payoff in the first 1-3 seconds** (Hook Point's 3-second world, Heath's Concrete + Unexpected). Show the keys/result or state the specific promise ("this 2-chord shift fixes your stiff left hand") before any intro. Deliver on it (Kane: hook then substance, not clickbait).
2. **Build for sends** (Berger Social Currency + Practical Value). Ask: who would this be sent to and what does it say about the sender? Write caption lines like "send to your practice buddy". Supported by Instagram naming sends as a top signal for non-followers (section 4).
3. **Pair the familiar with the surprising** (Thompson MAYA). Known song or common problem + unexpected angle, arrangement or fix.
4. **One reel, one idea, one outcome** (Miller clarity, Heath Simple). A single teachable move per reel; cut everything that does not advance it.
5. **Make a named, repeatable series** (Eyal triggers, Clear obvious cue and identity, Berger triggers). Fixed title pattern ("Chord Fix #14"), fixed visual template, fixed weekday, so the viewer's cue is recognisable and the next one is anticipated. Vary the content inside the template (variable reward).
6. **Teach, show process, minimise asks** (Kleon, Vaynerchuk jabs, Cialdini reciprocity). Many jabs; one simple right hook (sheet music, free PDF) occasionally.
7. **Pick a smallest viable audience and name it** (Godin). Speak to a defined learner type in the first line or caption.
8. **Publish a sustainable, consistent cadence** (Clear, Kleon, Vaynerchuk). Prefer a rate you can hold for months over bursts; batch to keep it easy. Buffer reports consistent posters get roughly 5x engagement per post (section 4, single source).
9. **Give viewers a small satisfying win and an easy next action** (Clear satisfying reward, Eyal investment, Cialdini commitment). End on the finished sound; prompt a tiny action such as save, or comment with their result.
10. **Differentiate with one visual/sound signature and refresh hooks** (Godin purple cow, Kane hook evolution). Examples: a consistent overlay style, a recognisable keyboard visual. Review which hook styles stop working and rotate.

### Gaps
- No book gives numeric posting cadence for modern Reels; cadence advice in rule 8 relies on one Buffer analysis.
- Ranking is judgement, not empirically tested.

## 4. Where book-era principles conflict with current short-form platform realities

### Takeaway
Most book principles hold as psychology, but several tactics are tied to older channels: link-driven "right hooks", scarcity/urgency gimmicks, face/personal-brand advice, static hook templates, and platform descriptions from 2013-2017. Instagram's current ranking emphasises watch time, likes and sends, rewards original content, and demotes reposts and watermarks.

### Cited Findings
- Mosseri (Jan 22, 2025, via Social Media Today): top three ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends; likes slightly matter more for connected (followers) reach, sends slightly more for unconnected (new) reach; creators should watch average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- Reels inputs include viewer's recent activity, history with the poster, content and early engagement; "Your Algorithm" controls launched on Reels in December 2025 — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/) (Oct 23, 2026)
- Originality: Instagram wants to give more distribution to original creators than reposters; reduced reach for aggregator accounts from April 2026; Reels with other apps' watermarks pushed down — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)
- Hashtags: Mosseri said they do not help reach; five-hashtag cap; Buffer recommends captions and profile keywords — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)
- Trial Reels show a reel to non-followers first, letting you test a format without exposing it to followers — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/) (Buffer does not attribute this to a dated Instagram statement)
- Cadence: Buffer's analysis of 100,000+ users found consistent posters get nearly 5x engagement per post; spreading posts out is advised over dumping many at once — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)
- Length is unresolved: Buffer's main article says short punchy clips tend to win, while a separate Buffer analysis teaser (Nov 12, 2026) says 90 seconds to 3 minutes Reels reached 4-7% more people than each account's own average — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)
- Vaynerchuk's Instagram guidance describes a 2013-era image-first platform with no links in posts — [IMPACT summary](https://www.impactplus.com/impact-book-summaries/jab-jab-jab-right-hook-gary-vaynerchuk)
- Crushing It's platform advice (Snapchat, Twitter, Facebook Live, YouTube as top wealth platform) is from a 2017-era platform landscape — [Life Club](https://lifeclub.org/books/crushing-it-gary-vaynerchuk-review-summary)
- Purple Cow's thesis that advertising is broken, and its pessimism about big companies being remarkable, have been challenged — [Brikman review](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/02/10/purple-cow/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Cow:_Transform_Your_Business_by_Being_Remarkable)
- Hooked: caveat that the model is a business-book framework without peer-reviewed support and does not address ethics of compulsive design — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/the-hooked-model/)

### Inferences
- **Hook Model investment/notifications:** an Instagram creator has no control over notifications or stored user data; the loop must be approximated by series cues and followed accounts. Ethics: avoid manipulative compulsion tactics.
- **Cialdini scarcity/authority:** fake scarcity damages trust; for a faceless account, authority must be shown through demonstrated playing/teaching quality and credentials in the bio, not a visible face.
- **Show Your Work / Crushing It personal brand:** both lean on the creator's person and story; a faceless account must move "story" to the music, the learner's journey and the process (hands, score, screen), which weakens liking/parasocial effects. Compensate with a consistent voice (captions), signature visuals and community replies.
- **Berger/Heath "tell a story" and Kane's 3-second hook:** a 3-second hook leaves little room for story setup; use in-medias-res (start at the payoff, then explain).
- **Sends vs. likes:** books emphasise sharing as general virality; the platform measures it as sends-per-reach, so optimise for DM-able content.
- **Hashtags and link-based right hooks:** older "jab, jab, jab, right hook" assumes tracking or links; on Reels the right hook is usually "link in bio", profile CTA, or follow-for-series, and hashtags are weak.
- **Length and trends:** a hit-driven distribution model (Thompson-style) is now mediated by recommendation systems; cross-posted TikTok clips with watermarks are penalised, which reinforces Vaynerchuk's "native" rule.
- **Weak evidence caution:** the Buffer and Social Media Today pieces are secondary reporting; Mosseri statements are dated 2025 and algorithm details change; no exact signal weights are public.

### Gaps
- No primary Instagram/Meta page was fetched; ranking facts are reported via Social Media Today and Buffer.
- No sourced data on retention thresholds, ideal reel length, or optimal posts per week for educational music accounts.
- No sourced data specifically on faceless accounts' performance versus face-led accounts.
- Whether Trial Reels are officially described the way Buffer states was not verified.
